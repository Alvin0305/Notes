    Make German manufacturing globally dominant using digital integration.

    🎯 PART 5 — Understanding Quality (Very Important)¶

    Now we enter a huge conceptual block.

    What is Quality?¶

    Word comes from Latin qualis → “how constituted”.

    Quality is not just “good product”.

    It depends on perspective.

    Different Definitions of Quality¶

    1️⃣ Conformance to Specifications¶

    Does the product meet design tolerances?

    Example:

    • A shaft diameter should be 10 ± 0.02 mm

    • If it is within this → good quality

    But…
    Meeting spec doesn’t always mean customer satisfaction.

    2️⃣ Fitness for Use¶

    Does it do its job well?

    Example:
    Both Mercedes and Renault Duster transport people.
    But if use = mountain driving?
    Then performance matters.

    This is user-based.

    3️⃣ Value for Price Paid¶

    Quality relative to cost.

    Cheap phone that works well → high perceived quality.

    4️⃣ Support Services¶

    After-sales service.
    Customer care.
    Maintenance support.

    Why is Maruti strong in India?
    Service network.

    5️⃣ Psychological Criteria¶

    Brand perception.

    Example:

    • Rolex

    • Mercedes-Benz

    Even before using them, we assume quality.

    📏 PART 6 — 8 Dimensions of Quality (Very Important)¶

    Given by David A. Garvin

    These 8 dimensions are conceptual gold.

    1. Performance – How well it functions

    2. Reliability – How often it fails

    3. Durability – How long it lasts

    4. Serviceability – Ease of repair

    5. Aesthetics – Look/feel

    6. Features – Extra characteristics

    7. Perceived Quality – Brand image

    8. Conformance – Meets standards

    🔍 Manufacturing vs Service Quality¶

    Manufacturing:

    • Measurable

    • Tangible

    • Tolerances

    • Defects

    Service:

    • Perception-based

    • Waiting time

    • Courtesy

    • Responsiveness

    🧮 PART 7 — Modern Definition of Quality¶

    This line is VERY IMPORTANT:

    Quality is inversely proportional to variability.

    Meaning:

    Less variation = Higher quality.

    If product performance fluctuates wildly → poor quality.

    🇯🇵 Ford Case Study (Extremely Important Concept)¶

    Ford compared US vs Japanese transmissions.

    Both met specifications.

    But:

    • US used 75% of tolerance band

    • Japan used only 25%

    Japanese product had:

    • Lower variability

    • Smoother shifting

    • Less noise

    • Fewer warranty claims

    Conclusion:
    Not just meeting spec — hitting target matters.

    This connects to the Sony case too.

    📺 Sony Case¶

    Sony USA:
    Uniform distribution inside specs.

    Sony Japan:
    Centered tightly around target.

    Customers preferred Japan.

    Lesson:
    Meeting target > Just meeting specification.

    💰 Cost of Quality (Extremely Important for Exams)¶

    Four categories:

    1️⃣ Prevention Costs
    2️⃣ Appraisal Costs
    3️⃣ Internal Failure Costs
    4️⃣ External Failure Costs

    Prevention Costs¶

    Avoid defects in first place.

    • Training

    • Quality planning

    • Design reviews

    Appraisal Costs¶

    Inspection & testing.

    Internal Failure Costs¶

    Scrap
    Rework
    Before delivery.

    External Failure Costs¶

    Warranty
    Customer complaints
    After delivery.

    Golden Concept:¶

    If you increase prevention cost,
    Internal and external failure costs reduce.

    That’s smart management.

    🔧 PART 8 — Reliability (New Concept Block)¶

    Now we shift from quality → reliability.

    Definition¶

    Reliability = Probability that product performs without failure for a specified time under stated conditions.

    Quality = Today
    Reliability = Future

    Important Difference¶

    Quality:
    Is product good right now?

    Reliability:
    Will it stay good over time?

    Reliability Metrics (Must Understand)¶

    1. PDF – Failure density

    2. CDF – Probability of failure till time t

    3. Reliability function R(t) – Probability of survival

    4. Hazard function h(t) – Instantaneous failure rate

    🛁 The Bathtub Curve (Super Important)¶

    Three phases:

    1️⃣ Infant mortality (Early failures)

    • High failure rate

    • Manufacturing defects

    • Burn-in period

    2️⃣ Useful life

    • Constant failure rate

    • Random failures

    • Modelled by exponential distribution

    3️⃣ Wear-out

    • Failure rate increases

    • Aging

    • Material fatigue

    📐 Exponential Distribution (Core Math)¶

    Used in useful life phase.

    If λ = failure rate

    PDF:
    \(f(t) = λe^{⁻λt}\) CDF:
    \(F(t) = 1 − e^{⁻λt}\) Reliability:
    \(R(t) = e^{⁻λt}\)

    \(MTTF = \frac{1}{λ}\) \(R(t) = e^{\frac{-t}{MTBF}}\) \(t = MTBF\times ln(\frac{1}{R})\) \(\lambda = \frac{1}{MTBF}\) \(MTBF = \frac{\text{total operating hours}}{\text{number of devices failed}} = \frac{1}{\lambda}\)

    🔗 System Reliability¶

    Series System¶

    All must work.

    Reliability:
    R = R1 × R2 × R3 × …

    Adding components reduces reliability.

    Parallel System¶

    At least one must work.

    Reliability:
    \(R_p = 1 − (1−R_1)(1−R_2)...(1 - R_n)\)

    Increases reliability.

    Reliability Prediction¶

    \[ \begin{aligned} R(s) &= R_a \times R_b \times R_c \times ... \times R_n \\ &= e^{-\lambda_at} \times e^{-\lambda_bt} \times e^{-\lambda_ct} \times ... \times e^{-\lambda_nt} \\ &= e^{-(\lambda_a + \lambda_b + ... + \lambda_n)t} \\ &= e^{-(\sum\lambda)t} \end{aligned} \]

    🧠 Concept That Matters Most¶

    In Industry 4.0:

    We don’t just manufacture.

    We:

    • Predict reliability

    • Predict warranty claims

    • Use data analytics

    • Use life-cycle modelling

    • Reduce variability digitally

    • Design reliability at concept stage

    Because:

    60–70% of life cycle cost is determined during design phase.

    That is HUGE.

    \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)dt &= 1 \\ p(a < t < b) &= \int_{a}^{b}f(t)dt \\ F(t) &= \int_{-\infty}^{t}f(t)dt \\ R(t) &= 1 - F(t) = \int_{t}^{\infty}f(t)dt \\ \text{Hazard Rate} = h(t) &= \frac{f(t)}{R(t)} = 1 - \frac{f(t)}{F(t)} \end{aligned} \]
