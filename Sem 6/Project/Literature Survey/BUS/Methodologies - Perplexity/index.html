    Methodologies Perplexity

    You can treat BUS as the OSDA‑SS “skeleton” and upgrade mainly the backbone and unknown‑class modeling: plug in a vision‑foundation‑model (VFM) segmentation backbone, adapt it with parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning (PEFT), and borrow prototype/similarity‑based tricks from recent UDA/UniDA/open‑world works to improve both common‑class mIoU and unknown‑class IoU beyond BUS.

    Below is a concrete methodology and a list of 2024+ papers with public GitHub repos you can reuse.

    What BUS already gives you¶

    BUS defines Open‑Set Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation (OSDA‑SS): labeled synthetic source (e.g., GTA5/SYNTHIA) and unlabeled real target (Cityscapes) with additional target‑private classes that must be rejected as “unknown.”arxiv It builds on DAFormer with a MiT‑B5 backbone and expands the segmentation head from CCC to C+1C+1C+1 channels so the extra channel models unknown pixels, trained via pseudo‑labels from an EMA teacher.

    Key components you should keep as the base:

    • Head expansion baseline: C+1C+1C+1‑way classifier with pseudo‑labels that assign low‑confidence pixels to the unknown channel.​

    • DECON loss: dilation–erosion–based contrastive loss that uses “erosion” features (confident private pixels) as positives and “dilated‑minus‑original” boundary features as negatives to sharpen common vs private boundaries.​

    • OpenReMix: class‑mix + size‑jitter mixing of “thing” classes from source into target, plus pasting target‑private regions into source images to better train the unknown head.​

    • Training recipe: DAFormer+MIC style (EMA teacher, DACS mixing, Rare Class Sampling, ImageNet Feature Distance, MobileSAM refinement), optimized for GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes.github+2​

    BUS reports large H‑Score gains vs DAFormer/HRDA/MIC, so you should treat this as your minimal baseline to beat in both H‑Score and common‑class mIoU.​

    Overall extension strategy¶

    1. Reproduce BUS exactly (same backbone, datasets, metrics) to get a trustable baseline on GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes.github​​

    2. Swap in a stronger VFM backbone (from Rein/FADA/vfm‑uda) while preserving the BUS OSDA‑SS losses and data pipeline.neurips+2

    3. Apply PEFT on the VFM (LoRA/adapters/prompt tuning) rather than full fine‑tuning, to keep foundation generalization while specializing to OSDA‑SS.github+2

    4. Enhance unknown‑class modeling using prototype & similarity ideas from UniMAP (UniDA‑SS) and Open‑World Semantic Segmentation Including Class Similarity.arxiv+3

    5. Run ablations to show incremental gains: BUS → BUS+VFM → BUS+VFM+PEFT → BUS+VFM+PEFT+prototype/similarity modules.

    Step 1: Reproduce BUS as your baseline¶

    • Use the official repo KU‑VGI/BUS and follow their configs for GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, including DAFormer+MiT‑B5, 40k iters, 512×512 crops, EMA, DACS, Rare Class Sampling, and MobileSAM refinement.openaccess.thecvf+2​

    • Verify you can match or get close to their reported common‑class mIoU and H‑Score (e.g., ~72.5% common mIoU and ~55.4% private IoU on GTA5→Cityscapes, leading to higher H‑Score than prior UDA baselines).​

    • Keep all OSDA‑SS details (unknown‑class definition, ignored labels, harmonic mean H‑Score metric) unchanged so improvements are comparable.​

    This gives you a clean “BUS (ours)” line to improve upon.

    Step 2: Replace the backbone with a VFM segmentation model¶

    2.1 Choose a VFM‑based segmentation backbone¶

    Recent works show strong gains when you reuse VFMs for DGSS/UDA:

    • Rein (“Stronger, Fewer, & Superior: Harnessing Vision Foundation Models for Domain Generalized Semantic Segmentation”, CVPR 2024): efficient robust fine‑tuning of VFMs for DGSS, achieving SOTA on Cityscapes and synthetic‑to‑real setups.github​

    • FADA (“Learning Frequency‑Adapted Vision Foundation Model for Domain Generalized Semantic Segmentation”, NeurIPS 2024): decomposes low/high‑frequency components to make VFMs more style‑invariant across domains.neurips​

    • vfm‑uda (“Exploring the Benefits of Vision Foundation Models for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation”, CVPR 2024 workshop): systematic analysis of VFMs in UDA for semantic segmentation.github​

    Concretely:

    • Pick a SegFormer/Mask2Former‑style VFM that Rein/FADA/vfm‑uda already use (e.g., ViT‑based, DINOv2‑ or MAE‑pretrained).github+2

    • Make sure it outputs multi‑scale feature maps compatible with DAFormer’s decoder; often you only need a small adapter to map backbone feature dimensions to DAFormer’s FPN levels.github+1​

    2.2 Integrate VFM into BUS¶

    • In the BUS codebase, replace the MiT‑B5 encoder with your chosen VFM encoder, keeping the DAFormer decoder and C+1C+1C+1 head unchanged.github+2​

    • Initialize the new backbone with its pretrained VFM weights (from ImageNet‑22k / COCO / large‑scale synthetic driving datasets) as released by Rein/FADA/vfm‑uda.neurips+2

    • Start with frozen early layers and fine‑tune only upper backbone blocks plus the decoder to avoid catastrophic forgetting of generic features, then later unfreeze more if needed.github+1

    This alone should boost common‑class mIoU vs BUS, since Rein/FADA report large gains in synthetic→real generalization even without explicit OSDA‑SS modeling.github+2

    Step 3: Use PEFT to adapt the VFM for OSDA‑SS¶

    Instead of full fine‑tuning, use PEFT modules on the VFM backbone so you keep foundation knowledge but specialize to OSDA‑SS.

    3.1 LoRA / adapter layers in the VFM¶

    • LoRA: insert low‑rank adapters on the attention and/or MLP projections in each transformer block of the VFM encoder; only these low‑rank matrices and normalization layers are trained in OSDA‑SS.github+2

    • Adapters: alternatively, add small bottleneck adapter MLPs after each block, as is standard in many VFM adaptation frameworks.github+1

    In practice:

    • Freeze the original backbone weights; train LoRA/adapter parameters plus the DAFormer decoder and the C+1C+1C+1 segmentation head.neurips+2

    • Keep BUS losses (source supervised, target pseudo‑label, DECON, OpenReMix‑driven supervision) unchanged, just backprop through the PEFT modules.​

    3.2 Prompt‑ or token‑based PEFT¶

    Rein introduces an efficient fine‑tuning strategy tailored for VFMs; its ideas are close to prompt‑like or token‑efficient adaptation.github​

    You can:

    • Follow Rein’s strategy of strong but parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning (e.g., tuning normalization/statistics, a few selected blocks, and lightweight heads) within the BUS framework instead of naive full fine‑tuning.github​

    • Optionally add visual prompts (extra learnable tokens) to the VFM encoder that are optimized for the target domain while the backbone remains mostly frozen.github+1

    This step is where you explicitly connect your work to “PEFT” in the paper title or problem statement.

    Step 4: Strengthen unknown‑class modeling with SOTA ideas¶

    BUS already improves unknown detection with DECON loss and OpenReMix, but you can add newer mechanisms that explicitly structure unknown feature space.

    4.1 Prototype‑based distinction (UniMAP‑style)¶

    UniMAP (CVPR 2025) introduces Universal Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation (UniDA‑SS) and proposes Domain‑Specific Prototype‑based Distinction (DSPD): per‑class, per‑domain prototypes that better separate common vs private categories.github+2

    To adapt this to OSDA‑SS:

    • Maintain source‑domain prototypes and target‑domain prototypes per known class in the VFM feature space.arxiv​

    • During DECON contrastive training, pull erosion‑mask (private) features away from both source and target prototypes of common classes, and encourage boundary features that look like common classes to align with their prototypes.arxiv​​

    • Use prototype similarity to refine pseudo‑labels near boundaries and for low‑confidence pixels (e.g., if a pixel is low‑confidence but near any common‑class prototype, reassign from unknown to that class; otherwise keep as unknown).arxiv​

    This combines BUS’s DECON idea with UniMAP’s prototype separation, which is explicitly designed for scenarios with unknown/target‑private classes.github+1

    4.2 Class‑similarity‑aware unknown handling¶

    “Open‑World Semantic Segmentation Including Class Similarity” (CVPR 2024) learns an open‑world segmentation model that can both segment known classes and separate unknowns while providing similarity scores from unknown clusters to known classes.arxiv+1

    You can:

    • Add an auxiliary head on top of VFM features that predicts an open‑world embedding, trained with their loss to distinguish known vs new classes.github+1

    • Use its unknown similarity scores as an extra criterion when deciding whether a pixel belongs to the BUS unknown head or a known class; for example, if energy/uncertainty is high but similarity to any known prototype is also high, treat it as a miscalibrated known pixel rather than unknown.arxiv+1

    This augments BUS’s hard threshold‑based unknown assignment with a semantically informed similarity score.

    4.3 Frequency‑aware regularization (FADA‑style)¶

    FADA’s frequency‑branch design separates low‑frequency content (geometry/semantics) from high‑frequency style, improving DGSS with VFMs.neurips​

    You can:

    • Add frequency‑token branches to the VFM backbone (or reuse FADA’s released code) for OSDA‑SS training so style shifts between GTA5/SYNTHIA and Cityscapes are better filtered out before BUS’s DECON/OpenReMix stages.neurips​

    • Apply DECON contrastive loss specifically to low‑frequency content features, which should further stabilize unknown boundaries under varying styles.neurips​

    Step 5: Training recipe, tuning, and ablations¶

    To make the improvements robust and publishable:

    • Keep the BUS pipeline: same data splits, ignored labels, unknown definition (thing classes removed from source, merged into a single unknown in target), H‑Score metric, and training schedule.​

    • Stage‑wise training:

      • Stage 1: train DGSS/UDA with the VFM backbone using Rein/FADA/vfm‑uda recipes (no OSDA yet), to get a strong closed‑set model.github+2

      • Stage 2: initialize BUS from Stage‑1 weights and train OSDA‑SS with DECON, OpenReMix, and your PEFT modules.​

      • Stage 3: add prototype/similarity heads (UniMAP/OWSS‑CS‑style) and fine‑tune lightly with smaller LR on PEFT parameters only.github+1

    • Hyperparameters:

      • Lower LR for VFM/PEFT modules than for DAFormer decoder and OSDA‑SS heads.github+1

      • Re‑tune pseudo‑label thresholds τp,τt\tau_p, \tau_tτp,τt because the VFM backbone is usually better calibrated than MiT‑B5; you may afford a higher threshold for unknown assignment.github​​

      • Balance new prototype/similarity losses with DECON and segmentation losses via grid search on a validation split.arxiv​​

    Run controlled ablations:

    1. BUS (MiT‑B5).

    2. BUS + VFM backbone (full fine‑tune).

    3. BUS + VFM + PEFT only.

    4. BUS + VFM + PEFT + UniMAP‑style prototypes.

    5. BUS + VFM + PEFT + OWSS‑CS similarity head + FADA‑style frequency tokens.

    Your target is consistent gains in H‑Score and common mIoU over BUS on both GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes.​

    Concrete experimental plan to beat BUS mIoU¶

    A practical path you can follow:

    1. Reimplement BUS baseline on GTA5→Cityscapes and SYNTHIA→Cityscapes using KU‑VGI/BUS.github​

    2. Swap MiT‑B5 for a VFM backbone from Rein (e.g., their best SegFormer‑VFM setting), adapting DAFormer to the new feature dimensions.github​

    3. Enable PEFT (LoRA/adapters) on the VFM backbone as in Rein/FADA, keeping most backbone weights frozen and training only PEFT modules, decoder, and segmentation heads.neurips+1

    4. Add UniMAP‑style prototypes on top of BUS’s DECON and OpenReMix to better separate common vs private features and refine pseudo‑labels near unknown boundaries.github+1

    5. Optionally integrate OWSS‑CS similarity scores as an auxiliary head to further reduce confusion between hard known pixels and unknowns.arxiv+1

    If you design ablations carefully and tune PEFT and thresholds, this combination (BUS OSDA‑SS framework + VFM+PEFT backbone + prototype/similarity enhancements) is a realistic route to achieving higher common‑class mIoU and H‑Score than the original BUS numbers.arxiv+3​
