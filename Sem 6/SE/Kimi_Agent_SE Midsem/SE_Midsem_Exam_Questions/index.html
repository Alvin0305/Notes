    Software Engineering - Midsem Exam Questions¶

    Based on the textbook "Object-Oriented Software Engineering: Practical Software Development using UML and Java" by Timothy C. Lethbridge and Robert Laganière

    1. SE INTRODUCTION¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. Define Software Engineering. What makes it different from programming?

    2. List and briefly explain the four main activities of software engineering mentioned in the textbook.

    3. What are the three main types of software systems? Give one example of each.

    4. Explain the difference between a customer and a user in software engineering context.

    5. What is the 80-20 rule (Pareto Principle) and how does it apply to software engineering?

    Long Answer Questions (5-7 marks)¶

    1. Discuss the importance of understanding the customer and user in software engineering. What techniques can be used to gather information about their needs?

    2. Explain why software engineering is considered a labor-intensive activity. What are the implications of this for project management?

    3. Describe the nine themes that are incorporated throughout the textbook for effective software engineering.

    2. EVOLUTION OF SOFTWARE ENGINEERING TECHNIQUES¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What was the "software crisis" that led to the development of software engineering as a discipline?

    2. How has the focus of software engineering evolved from the 1960s to the present day?

    3. What is the difference between structured programming and object-oriented programming?

    4. Explain the shift from waterfall to iterative/agile approaches in software development.

    Long Answer Questions (5-7 marks)¶

    1. Trace the evolution of software development methodologies from the early days to modern agile approaches. What factors drove these changes?

    2. Discuss how the introduction of UML changed software engineering practices. What problems did it solve?

    3. Compare and contrast the traditional "opportunistic approach" with modern software engineering practices. What are the problems with the opportunistic approach?

    3. SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT LIFE CYCLE (SDLC)¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. List the phases of the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) in their typical order.

    2. What is the purpose of the requirements gathering phase in SDLC?

    3. Why is the maintenance phase considered the most expensive phase of the SDLC?

    4. What is verification and validation (V&V) and when is it performed in the SDLC?

    Long Answer Questions (5-7 marks)¶

    1. Describe the complete Software Development Life Cycle. Explain what activities are performed in each phase and how they relate to each other.

    2. Explain the concept of traceability in the context of SDLC. Why is it important?

    3. Discuss why requirements changes are inevitable in software development. How should a software engineer handle these changes throughout the SDLC?

    4. PROCESS MODELS¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    Waterfall Model¶

    1. Draw and label the waterfall model. List its phases in order.

    2. What are the main limitations of the waterfall model?

    3. When is the waterfall model most appropriate?

    Iterative Waterfall Model¶

    1. How does the iterative waterfall model differ from the pure waterfall model?

    2. What is the advantage of allowing feedback loops between phases in the iterative waterfall model?

    Prototyping Model¶

    1. What is a prototype in software development? What are the two types of prototypes?

    2. What is the main purpose of prototyping?

    3. Explain the difference between throwaway prototyping and evolutionary prototyping.

    Incremental Model¶

    1. What is incremental development? How does it differ from the waterfall model?

    2. What are the advantages of the incremental model?

    V-Model¶

    1. Draw the V-model and explain its structure.

    2. What is the relationship between the left side and right side of the V-model?

    Evolutionary/Spiral Model¶

    1. Who developed the spiral model? What are its four quadrants?

    2. What is risk analysis and why is it important in the spiral model?

    3. Explain Fred Brooks' famous quotation: "The question is not whether to build a pilot system and throw it away. You will do that. The question is whether to plan in advance to build a throwaway."

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Compare and contrast the Waterfall model and the Spiral model. What are the advantages and disadvantages of each? When would you use one over the other?

    2. Explain the Phased-Release model. How does it address some of the problems of the waterfall model?

    3. Describe the Evolutionary model. What does it show that is not clear from the spiral model?

    4. Discuss the Concurrent Engineering model. How does it account for the "divide and conquer" principle?

    5. What is eXtreme Programming (XP)? List and explain at least five of its key tenets.

    6. Compare the Agile approach with traditional process models. What are the advantages and drawbacks of agile methods?

    7. Explain how you would choose a process model for a particular project. What factors would you consider?

    5. FEASIBILITY STUDY¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is a feasibility study? What are its main objectives?

    2. List the three main types of feasibility that should be considered.

    3. What is technical feasibility? Give an example of a project that might be technically infeasible.

    4. What is economic feasibility? How is it determined?

    5. What is schedule feasibility?

    Long Answer Questions (5-7 marks)¶

    1. Describe the components of a feasibility study. What should be included in a feasibility study report?

    2. Explain how you would conduct a feasibility study for a proposed software system. What steps would you follow?

    3. Discuss the importance of feasibility study in the software development process. What happens if a feasibility study is not conducted properly?

    6. REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is requirement analysis? Why is it important?

    2. List four techniques for gathering requirements.

    3. What is domain analysis? Why should it be performed before gathering requirements?

    4. What is the difference between functional and non-functional requirements? Give two examples of each.

    5. What are quality requirements? Give three examples.

    6. What is the difference between a feature and a requirement?

    7. What is gold-plating? Why should it be avoided?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the process of requirements gathering. What techniques can be used and when is each most appropriate?

    2. Explain the principles for gathering good requirements as discussed in the textbook.

    3. Discuss the different types of requirements (functional, quality, platform, process). Why is it important to distinguish between them?

    4. What are the common problems in requirements gathering? How can they be addressed?

    5. Explain the concept of scope creep. How can it be prevented or managed?

    7. SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS SPECIFICATION (SRS)¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is an SRS document? What is its purpose?

    2. List five characteristics of a good requirement (the "quality criteria" for requirements).

    3. What does it mean for a requirement to be verifiable? Give an example of a requirement that is NOT verifiable.

    4. What is traceability in the context of requirements?

    5. What is a use case? How does it relate to requirements?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the typical contents of an SRS document. What sections should it contain?

    2. Explain the principles for writing good requirements documents as discussed in the textbook.

    3. What is the difference between user requirements and system requirements? How are they documented differently?

    4. Discuss the importance of requirements review. What should be checked during a requirements review?

    5. Explain how requirements should be organized in an SRS document for maximum clarity and usability.

    8. REQUIREMENT MODELING¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is requirement modeling? Why is it important?

    2. List three types of models that can be used for requirement modeling.

    3. What is the purpose of a domain model?

    4. What is the difference between analysis and design in the context of modeling?

    5. What is OCL (Object Constraint Language)? What is it used for?

    Long Answer Questions (5-7 marks)¶

    1. Describe how UML can be used for requirement modeling. What diagrams are most useful at this stage?

    2. Explain the process of creating a domain model. What elements should be included?

    3. Discuss the relationship between requirements and models. How do models help in understanding and communicating requirements?

    9. USE CASE DIAGRAM¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is a use case diagram? What does it show?

    2. Draw the UML notation for:

    3. An actor
    4. A use case
    5. An association between actor and use case
    6. An «include» relationship

    7. An «extend» relationship

    8. What is the difference between «include» and «extend» relationships? Give an example of each.

    9. What is a generalized actor? How is it represented?

    10. What is a system boundary in a use case diagram?

    11. What are the guidelines for naming use cases?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the components of a use case diagram. Explain each component with examples.

    2. Explain how to identify actors for a system. What questions should you ask?

    3. What is the relationship between use cases and requirements? How do use cases help in requirements gathering?

    4. Draw a use case diagram for the following scenarios:

      • A library management system
      • An ATM system
      • An online shopping system

    5. Explain the concept of use case generalization. When would you use it?

    6. Discuss common mistakes made when creating use case diagrams. How can they be avoided?

    10. SEQUENCE DIAGRAM¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is a sequence diagram? What does it show?

    2. Draw and label the main elements of a sequence diagram.

    3. What is a lifeline in a sequence diagram?

    4. What is an activation box? What does it represent?

    5. What is the difference between a synchronous and asynchronous message? How are they represented differently?

    6. What is a combined fragment? List three types of interaction operators.

    7. What is object destruction? How is it shown in a sequence diagram?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the components of a sequence diagram. Explain how to read and create one.

    2. Explain the different types of messages that can appear in a sequence diagram.

    3. What are combined fragments? Describe at least four types of interaction operators (e.g., loop, alt, opt, par) and when to use each.

    4. Draw a sequence diagram for the following scenarios:

      • A student registering for a course
      • A customer withdrawing money from an ATM
      • A user sending a message in a chat application

    5. Explain the difference between a sequence diagram and a communication diagram. When would you use each?

    6. Discuss how sequence diagrams can be used for requirements analysis and design.

    11. STATE DIAGRAM¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is a state diagram? What does it show?

    2. Draw and label the main elements of a state diagram.

    3. What is the difference between a state and an activity?

    4. What is a transition? What are its components?

    5. What is a guard condition? How is it represented?

    6. What is a self-transition? Give an example.

    7. What are entry and exit actions? How are they specified?

    8. What is a composite state (superstate)? What are its advantages?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the components of a state diagram. Explain each element with examples.

    2. Explain the difference between Moore and Mealy machines. Which one does UML state diagrams follow?

    3. Draw a state diagram for the following:

      • A traffic light
      • A vending machine
      • An elevator
      • A login process

    4. What is the difference between a state diagram and an activity diagram? When would you use each?

    5. Explain how state diagrams can be used to model complex behavior. Give an example.

    6. Discuss the relationship between state diagrams and sequence diagrams. How do they complement each other?

    12. ACTIVITY DIAGRAM¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is an activity diagram? What does it show?

    2. Draw and label the main elements of an activity diagram.

    3. What is the difference between an action and an activity?

    4. What is a fork node? What is a join node?

    5. What is a decision node? How is it different from a merge node?

    6. What are swimlanes? What is their purpose?

    7. What is a signal sending and signal receipt? How are they represented?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the components of an activity diagram. Explain each element with examples.

    2. Explain the concept of swimlanes. How do they help in modeling business processes?

    3. Draw an activity diagram for the following:

      • The process of ordering a product online
      • The process of checking out a book from a library
      • A software build and deployment process

    4. Compare activity diagrams with flowcharts. What are the advantages of activity diagrams?

    5. Explain how activity diagrams can be used for modeling both sequential and parallel activities.

    6. Discuss the relationship between activity diagrams and use cases. How do they complement each other?

    13. UML DIAGRAMS (OVERVIEW)¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is UML? What is its purpose?

    2. List the 13 types of UML diagrams and categorize them as structural or behavioral.

    3. What is the difference between a structural diagram and a behavioral diagram?

    4. What is the purpose of a package diagram?

    5. What is a deployment diagram? What does it show?

    6. What is a component diagram?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the different types of UML diagrams. For each, explain its purpose and when it should be used.

    2. Explain how the different UML diagrams work together to provide a complete model of a system.

    3. Discuss the importance of visual modeling in software engineering. What are the benefits of using UML?

    4. What is the relationship between UML and the software development process? At what stages are different diagrams most useful?

    14. RELATIONSHIPS (ASSOCIATION, AGGREGATION, ETC.)¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    1. What is an association in UML? How is it represented?

    2. What is multiplicity? List the common multiplicity values and their meanings.

    3. What is the difference between unidirectional and bidirectional associations?

    4. What is an association class? When is it needed?

    5. What is aggregation? How is it different from a regular association?

    6. What is composition? How is it different from aggregation?

    7. What is generalization? How is it represented in UML?

    8. What is the difference between generalization and inheritance?

    9. What is a dependency relationship? When is it used?

    10. What is a realization relationship? Give an example.

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Compare and contrast association, aggregation, and composition. Give examples of each and explain when to use them.

    2. Explain the concept of multiplicity in detail. How do you determine the appropriate multiplicity for a relationship?

    3. Describe the different types of relationships that can exist between classes in UML. For each, explain its semantics and notation.

    4. Explain the concept of navigability in associations. What are the implications of navigability for implementation?

    5. Draw a class diagram showing the following relationships:

      • A University has many Departments (composition)
      • A Department has many Courses (composition)
      • A Student is enrolled in many Courses (association)
      • A Professor teaches many Courses (association)
      • A TeachingAssistant is a type of Student (generalization)

    6. Discuss the importance of correctly identifying relationships when creating class diagrams. What problems can arise from incorrect relationship modeling?

    15. DESIGN PATTERNS (FACADE, OBSERVER, ADAPTER)¶

    Short Answer Questions (2-3 marks)¶

    General Design Patterns¶

    1. What is a design pattern? What are the three main categories of design patterns?

    2. What are the benefits of using design patterns?

    3. What is the difference between a design pattern and a framework?

    Facade Pattern¶

    1. What is the Facade pattern? What problem does it solve?

    2. What is the intent of the Facade pattern?

    3. Give an example scenario where the Facade pattern would be useful.

    4. How does the Facade pattern reduce coupling?

    Observer Pattern¶

    1. What is the Observer pattern? What problem does it solve?

    2. What are the main components of the Observer pattern?

    3. Give a real-world example of the Observer pattern.

    4. How does the Observer pattern reduce coupling?

    5. What is the difference between push and pull models in the Observer pattern?

    Adapter Pattern¶

    1. What is the Adapter pattern? What problem does it solve?

    2. What are the two types of Adapter pattern? Explain each.

    3. Give an example scenario where the Adapter pattern would be useful.

    4. How does the Adapter pattern improve reuse and flexibility?

    Long Answer Questions (5-10 marks)¶

    1. Describe the Facade pattern in detail. Include:

      • Its intent
      • When to use it
      • Its structure (participants)
      • An example implementation
      • Benefits and drawbacks

    2. Describe the Observer pattern in detail. Include:

      • Its intent
      • When to use it
      • Its structure (participants)
      • An example implementation
      • Benefits and drawbacks

    3. Describe the Adapter pattern in detail. Include:

      • Its intent
      • When to use it
      • Its structure (participants)
      • An example implementation
      • Benefits and drawbacks

    4. Compare and contrast the Facade, Observer, and Adapter patterns. What are the similarities and differences? When would you use each?

    5. Explain how design patterns promote good software design principles such as low coupling and high cohesion.

    6. Draw a class diagram showing the structure of:

      • The Facade pattern
      • The Observer pattern
      • The Adapter pattern

    7. Discuss how the Observer pattern is used in the Model-View-Controller (MVC) architecture.

    8. Explain how the Adapter pattern can be used to integrate legacy code with new systems.

    BONUS: COMPREHENSIVE QUESTIONS¶

    Long Answer Questions (10-15 marks)¶

    1. You are asked to develop a new online banking system. Describe:
    2. How you would conduct the feasibility study
    3. What process model you would choose and why
    4. How you would gather and document requirements
    5. What UML diagrams you would create and why

    6. What design patterns might be useful and where

    7. Compare and contrast all the process models discussed in the textbook (Waterfall, Iterative Waterfall, Prototyping, Incremental, V-Model, Spiral, Evolutionary, Agile). For each, discuss:

    8. Its structure
    9. Its advantages
    10. Its disadvantages

    11. When it is most appropriate

    12. Explain the complete process of requirements engineering from initial stakeholder meetings to the final SRS document. What techniques, tools, and deliverables are involved at each stage?

    13. Design a complete UML model for a system of your choice (e.g., hospital management system, online examination system, hotel reservation system). Include:

    14. Use case diagram
    15. Class diagram with all relationships
    16. Sequence diagram for a key scenario

    ANSWER GUIDELINES¶

    Key Points to Include in Answers¶

    SE Introduction¶

    • Software engineering is about solving customer problems, not just writing code
    • Four main activities: requirements, design, implementation, verification
    • Importance of customers vs. users

    Process Models¶

    • Waterfall: Sequential, rigid, good for well-understood projects
    • Iterative: Allows feedback between phases
    • Prototyping: For exploring requirements
    • Incremental: Deliver in parts
    • Spiral: Risk-driven, iterative with explicit risk analysis
    • Agile: Lightweight, flexible, customer-focused

    Requirements¶

    • Functional vs. non-functional requirements
    • Use cases capture functional requirements
    • SRS should be clear, unambiguous, verifiable, traceable

    UML Diagrams¶

    • Use case: System functionality from user perspective
    • Class: Static structure with relationships
    • Sequence: Object interactions over time
    • State: Object lifecycle
    • Activity: Workflow/process flow

    Relationships¶

    • Association: "knows a" relationship
    • Aggregation: "has a" (whole-part, parts can exist independently)
    • Composition: "contains a" (whole-part, parts cannot exist independently)
    • Generalization: "is a" relationship (inheritance)

    Design Patterns¶

    • Facade: Simplified interface to a complex subsystem
    • Observer: One-to-many dependency, notification mechanism
    • Adapter: Convert one interface to another
