    Ownership of Business

    These notes provide a comprehensive and detailed overview of the ownership and forms of business organizations as presented in "2B_OWNERSHIP OF BUSINESS.pdf".

    I. Introduction to Business Ownership¶

    There are five primary forms of business organizations. These are categorized into non-professional and professional organizations:

    • Non-Professional: Sole Proprietorship, Partnership, and Joint Hindu Family Business.
    • Professional: Joint Stock Company and Cooperative Societies.

    II. Sole Proprietorship¶

    A business owned, managed, and controlled by a single individual who bears all risks and receives all profits. It is the simplest form of organization and is most suitable for small businesses and personalized services like beauty parlors or retail shops.

    1. Key Features¶

    • Formation and Closure: Easy to start and close with minimal legal formalities; no separate law governs it.
    • Unlimited Liability: The owner is personally responsible for debts; personal assets (e.g., a car) can be sold to repay business liabilities.
    • Sole Risk Bearer: The owner alone bears the risk of failure but also enjoys 100% of the profits.
    • Control: Absolute decision-making power rests with the owner without interference.
    • No Separate Entity: Law makes no distinction between the owner and the business.
    • Lack of Continuity: Death, insanity, or bankruptcy of the owner can lead to business closure.

    2. Merits and Limitations¶

    • Merits: Prompt decision-making, confidentiality of information, direct incentive to work hard, and a sense of personal accomplishment.
    • Limitations: Resources are limited to personal savings/borrowings, limited life span, and the owner may lack expertise in all managerial areas (purchasing, selling, financing).

    III. Partnership¶

    An association of two or more persons (minimum 2, maximum 50) who agree to carry on a business and share profits/losses. It is governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932.

    1. Key Features¶

    • Formation: Based on a legal agreement (Partnership Deed) for a lawful business with a profit motive.
    • Liability: Partners have unlimited joint and several liability, meaning each partner can be held responsible for the firm's entire debt.
    • Mutual Agency: Every partner is both an agent (represents others) and a principal (bound by others' acts).
    • Shared Control: Decisions are generally made through mutual consent and joint efforts.

    2. Types of Partners¶

    • Active: Contributes capital, manages the firm, and has unlimited liability.
    • Sleeping/Dormant: Contributes capital and shares profits but does not participate in management.
    • Secret: Association with the firm is unknown to the public; otherwise, functions like an active partner.
    • Nominal: Allows the use of their name; does not contribute capital or manage, but is liable to third parties.
    • Minor Partner: Cannot be a full partner but can be admitted to the benefits of the firm with consent. Their liability is limited to their capital contribution, and they do not manage the firm.

    3. Merits and Limitations¶

    • Merits: Easy formation/closure, balanced decision-making through specialization, more funds than a sole proprietor, and shared risk.
    • Limitations: Unlimited liability, potential for conflicts between partners, lack of business continuity, and low public confidence (as accounts are not public).

    IV. Joint Hindu Family Business (HUF)¶

    A unique Indian form of business owned and carried on by members of a Hindu Undivided Family.

    1. Key Features¶

    • Membership: Acquired by birth; three successive generations can be members (co-parceners).
    • Karta: The eldest male (or female per 2005 amendment) member who has absolute control and unlimited liability.
    • Liability of Members: Co-parceners (members other than Karta) have limited liability up to their share in the property.
    • Continuity: Stable; the death of the Karta does not end the business as the next eldest takes over.

    2. Merits and Limitations¶

    • Merits: Effective control by Karta, stability, and high loyalty among family members.
    • Limitations: Resources limited to ancestral property, Karta is overburdened with liability/management, and the decline of joint families affects this model.

    V. Joint Stock Company¶

    A voluntary association with a separate legal entity, perpetual succession, and a common seal. It is governed by The Companies Act, 2013.

    1. Key Features¶

    • Artificial Person: Created by law; it can own property and enter contracts but cannot breathe or talk.
    • Separate Legal Entity: Distinct from its owners (shareholders).
    • Perpetual Succession: "Members may come and go, but the company continues" until legally wound up.
    • Limited Liability: Shareholders are only liable up to the unpaid amount on their shares.
    • Management: Controlled by an elected Board of Directors.

    2. Private vs. Public Companies¶

    Feature Private Limited Company Public Limited Company
    Members Min 2 / Max 200 Min 7 / Max Unlimited
    Share Transfer Restricted among members Freely transferable 24/7
    Public Invitation Prohibited Allowed (Prospectus required)
    Directors Minimum 2 Minimum 3
    Name Clause Must end with "Pvt Ltd" Must end with "Ltd"

    3. Formation and Documents¶

    Formation is a complex, expensive process involving regulators like SEBI and the Registrar of Companies (ROC).

    • Memorandum of Association (MOA): The fundamental document containing five clauses: Name, Location, Objective, Liability, and Capital.
    • Articles of Association (AOA): Internal rules for share transfer, voting, meetings, and director appointments.

    Charateristics of company: 1. Huge capital 2. Stability of business 3. Economies of scale 4. Scope of expansion 5. Professional management 6. Tax benefits 7. Risk diffusion: (by large number of shareholders. They need to bear risk only up to the extent of amount of shares).

    VI. Cooperative Society¶

    A voluntary association managed democratically for the benefit of its members to achieve common economic goals.

    • Governance: Managed by an elected committee.
    • Characteristics: Separate legal status, stable existence, and limited liability for members.
    • Example: Amul, which grew from a small group of farmers in 1946 to a massive network.

    VII. Anatomy of an Industrial Unit¶

    An industrial unit is divided into four main quadrants surrounding Corporate Management (Planning, Organizing, Leading, Controlling):

    1. Finance: Budgeting, capital management, and accounting.
    2. Production: Plant maintenance, quality control, and safety.
    3. Personnel: Recruitment, training, and labor relations.
    4. Marketing: Demand forecasting, distribution, and advertising.
